2027 Campaign Manifesto.

The commitments for a second term, in the manifesto's own words.

Introduction

From Reforms to Prosperity: Agenda 2027–2031.

The APC manifesto for the 2027 presidential election, under the theme “A Nigeria That Works for Everyone: Peace, Opportunity, Dignity.” Every quotation below is word for word, with its page number.

“Our central promise for 2027-2031 is national prosperity: an economy whose progress is felt not only in reforms, but in the homes, markets, farms, factories and workplaces of Nigerians every day.” — p. 16

“The first term rescued the economy from old instabilities and rebuilt the foundations. The second term will turn stronger foundations into visible results in every state, local government, ward, and community.” — p. 16

Four pillars, eight priorities. Secure and Resilient Nation; Better Quality and More Affordable Life for Every Nigerian; Energy, Infrastructure & Economic Growth; and Effective and Accountable Governance.

Economic priorities

Published commitment

“The new growth model must be organised around productive capital, competitive Nigerian enterprise, industrial scale and exports that earn foreign exchange.” — p. 74

“Expanded Jobs & Higher Real Incomes: Annual economic growth accelerated to 6 per cent, youth unemployment halved, and equitable credit reached half of all economically active Nigerians.” — p. 18

“Single-digit inflation, at most 8% year-on-year by end-2031; subject to credible, independent monetary policy.” — p. 79

National infrastructure

Published commitment

“We will complete and maintain strategically important highway and railway corridors linking agro-industrial belts, mining centres, manufacturing anchors, major population centres and border crossings.” — p. 76

“Power must stop being a binding constraint on factories, exports, investment and household welfare.” — p. 73

“Available power generation scaled towards 10,000 MW, metering extended towards full coverage, transmission and distribution networks reinforced, and clean energy access extended to underserved communities.” — p. 18

No supporter proposals have been submitted for this topic yet.

Security

Published commitment

“Complete the establishment and equipping of the four new Army divisions in Makurdi, Ilorin, Jalingo and Benin City, recruit and train the 28,000 additional personnel approved, and report publicly on their deployment.” — p. 26

“Train and deploy the 50,000 newly recruited officers and expand recruitment and tactical training towards a 1:400 police-to-citizen ratio, maximising visible patrols across residential neighbourhoods, commercial centres, schools, and transit corridors.” — p. 27

“Complete e-surveillance coverage of all mapped borders (31 of 190 mapped areas remain), extend e-Gates, passenger-data (APIS) and MIDAS border systems to more entry points, and strengthen patrol, screening and intelligence-sharing.” — p. 28

Proposed, not official

No supporter proposals have been submitted for this topic yet.

Education

Published commitment

“Scale HOPE-EDU nationwide to halve the number of out-of-school children by 2031, extend AGILE (now in 18 states) to further states where the number of out-of-school girls is highest, and establish dedicated return-to-school pathways for teenage mothers.” — p. 41

“Building on the 80,284 teachers already certified and licensed, recruit and train 20,000 additional teachers, prioritising female educators and underserved rural communities nationwide.” — p. 41

“Scale NELFUND, expand the National Smart Learning Initiative, sustain industrial peace in public universities, and extend the Student Venture Capital Grant and Diaspora BRIDGE to additional tertiary institutions.” — p. 42

Proposed, not official

No supporter proposals have been submitted for this topic yet.

Healthcare

Published commitment

“Expand active health insurance coverage from more than 23 million enrollees to 40 per cent of Nigerians by 2031, subsidising poor and vulnerable Nigerians through the Vulnerable Group Fund, and cut out-of-pocket spending from 71 per cent to below 50 per cent of health expenditure.” — p. 43

“Expand static and mobile primary care, upgrade secondary facilities across all local government areas, and strengthen referral networks to tertiary hospitals, raising the share of functional primary health centres from 53 per cent today towards 90 per cent by 2031 and extending direct quarterly financing under the Basic Health Care Provision Fund from about 8,300 to all 17,600 primary health centres.” — p. 43

Proposed, not official

No supporter proposals have been submitted for this topic yet.

Agriculture

Published commitment

“Steady staple-food prices by building on the Guaranteed Minimum Price scheme and the strategic grain reserve, with published reserve levels and releases, and publish a monthly staple-food price tracker alongside headline inflation so that families and farmers can see what is changing.” — p. 47

“Cut waste and cost between the farm and the table through the storage, cold-chain, farm-to-market road and agro-processing commitments in Pillar 3, so that harvests reach markets fresh and fewer are lost along the way.” — p. 47

“Storage up 50 per cent by 2028; post-harvest losses halved by 2030” — p. 79

Youth and employment

Published commitment

“Execute a comprehensive national youth employment strategy with a published baseline and annual targets, aiming to halve youth unemployment by 2031 and to report each year the share of new labour-market entrants who find work.” — p. 44

“Expand skills training to two million young citizens, scale NiYA and Digital Literacy for All (748,988 trained to date) toward 30 million Nigerians, and publish NYSC private-sector placement performance data, making job placement and private-sector partnership the test of every skilling programme.” — p. 44

“one million export-linked jobs for young Nigerians through talent and services exports by 2030” — p. 18
